# Sales Handover and Loom Script templates

Both documents must prove the redesign will convert better, using **evidence from the two pages**: quote the old wording from `audit.md` beside the new wording, and name the conversion principle (from the CRO playbook) behind each change. Only describe what is actually in the new design.

---

## A. Sales Handover — "[Business] (Cold Email Lead).pdf"

Vertical format, no tables (never the old three-column table). 6–9 blocks in page order. Always include blocks for: Hero, CTAs / header, Trust & social proof, and Final CTA. Choose the rest from the most meaningful strategic changes: hero messaging, navigation, trust positioning, services presentation, social proof, information hierarchy, conversion pathways, CTA strategy, lead capture, mobile UX, business-critical information, content and messaging, visual hierarchy, brand continuity, user journey. The aim: a salesperson can confidently explain what changed → why → how it benefits the prospect, instead of just saying it "looks better".

```
[Element Name]

What Was Changed:
Before: "[exact old heading / CTA text]" ([where it was, e.g. footer only, below the fold])
After: "[exact new heading / CTA text]" ([where it is now])
[One or two sentences on what else changed in this section.]

The CRO Strategy (The Why):
[2–3 sentences. Name the principle, e.g. social proof, choice reduction, friction reduction, and explain how it changes visitor behaviour for this industry.]

Pitch Talking Point:
"[One plain-English sentence the salesperson can say to the prospect.]"
```

End with:

```
CTA Comparison
Before: [number] calls to action. [List with positions, e.g. "Submit" (contact form, bottom of page).]
After: [number] calls to action. Primary: "[text]" in header, hero and final section. Secondary: "[text]". Service micro-CTAs: "[text]", "[text]", "[text]".

Trust & Social Proof
Before: [what was there]
After: [what is now shown, with sources] — or — [placeholder for Google reviews, to be filled with the client's real reviews]
```

## B. Loom Script — "[Business] - Loom Script.pdf"

Always delivered as a PDF, together with the Before & After image (Ben/Brendan read it while the Before & After is on screen).

For Ben or Brendan, recorded over the Before & After image. 600–700 words, about 4.5–5 minutes. Conversational Australian English. Every section must point at something visible on screen.

```
Target Prospect: [first name]
Company: [business name] ([industry])
Estimated length: ~4:30–5:00

[0:00 – 0:40] Part 1: The hook
[Screen: full Before & After, current site left, new design right]
"Hi [first name], Ben here from Sites n Stores. Hope you're well."
- One specific, genuine compliment about the business (from their site).
- The honest gap: the current site isn't turning visitors into [their primary action] as well as it could.
- "This isn't just a prettier version. Every change is based on how [industry] customers actually decide, so more of your visitors [book / call / request a quote]."

[0:40 – 1:40] Part 2: First impression and the main call to action
[Screen: zoom on both heroes]
- Read the old headline and old button text, then the new ones (bold the new text).
- Explain the 5-second test: a visitor now knows what you do, who it's for and what to do next.
- Point to the header button and phone: "Whatever page they're on, [action] is one click away."
- Name the principle in plain words (clarity, one clear path, fewer choices).

[1:40 – 3:00] Part 3: Trust and social proof
[Screen: trust bar, then reviews section]
- Walk through the trust bar: what it shows and why it's high on the page.
- Reviews: read one real review excerpt with the first name and rating, and explain why proof next to the button lifts enquiries.
  If the section is a placeholder: "This is where your best Google reviews go. Customers trust other customers more than anything we can write, so we'd pull your real reviews in here."
- Credibility facts (years, licences, projects) and why they matter to this buyer.

[3:00 – 4:00] Part 4: The flow that leads to an enquiry
[Screen: scroll the new design top to bottom]
- Services rewritten around outcomes, each with its own button (quote the micro-CTAs).
- "How it works" steps: removes the "what happens next?" worry that stops people enquiring.
- FAQ: answers objections before they become reasons to leave.
- Compare: "On the current site, the only way to get in touch is [old CTA, where]. On the new design there are [n] clear next steps all the way down, each worded for exactly what the visitor is reading." Quote 2–3 of the new button texts.

[4:00 – 4:50] Part 5: The close
[Screen: final CTA section, then calendar]
- The final section: short form plus phone, the easiest possible next step.
- "Look, this design is about making sure your website works as hard as you do. I'd love to hear what you think. My calendar will pop up at the end of this video, so pick a time that suits you, or just reply to this email. Either way, I look forward to chatting. Take care!"
```

Rules:
- Quote real text from both designs in **bold**; never describe a section that isn't in the design.
- Name 3–4 conversion principles in plain English (no jargon dumps).
- Never promise a specific uplift percentage. Say "designed to turn more visitors into [action]".
- Never mention reviews or numbers that aren't in the design.
