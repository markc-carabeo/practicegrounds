# CRO Playbook for Homepage Redesigns

Use this to choose the primary action, the section flow, the CTA wording and the trust signals. Confirm the industry defaults with 1–2 quick web searches each run; prospects differ.

## 1. Pick the primary action

| Buyer situation | Primary action | Secondary action |
|---|---|---|
| Local trade / urgent or site-based work (surveying, plumbing, electrical, building, cleaning, pest) | Get a Quote / Request a Free Quote | Call [number] (click-to-call) |
| Health, allied health, psychology, dental, physio | Book an Appointment / Book Your First Session | Call the clinic / Check availability |
| Professional services (legal, accounting, advisory, coaching) | Book a Free Consultation / Book a 15-Min Call | Download a guide / See how it works |
| B2B, SaaS, platforms, enterprise | Book a Demo | Explore Solutions / See Pricing |
| Retail, e-commerce, product brands | Shop [Category] / Shop Now | View best sellers / Find a store |
| Hospitality, venues, events | Book a Table / Check Dates | View menu / Call us |
| Education, training, childcare | Enrol Now / Book a Tour | Download the prospectus / Check intake dates |
| Industrial, waste, logistics, B2B services | Book a Meeting / Request a Quote | Register your [material/site] |
| Food & drink producers selling to restaurants and shoppers (charcuterie, cheese, bakeries, coffee roasters, wineries) | Wholesale enquiry, e.g. Put [Brand] on Your Menu | Find a Stockist |

One primary action (the goal), but **not one repeated phrase**. Keep a short, steady anchor button in the header (e.g. "Get a Quote"). Every other button gets its own creative, section-specific wording that leads to the same goal (see §2).

## 2. Writing CTAs

**Make them creative and varied.** A page where every button says "Get a Quote" feels lazy and gets ignored. Each button should name the exact next step the visitor wants *at that point on the page*, in their language, while still leading to the primary goal (the quote form, booking or a call).

Example (construction surveyor): header "Get a Quote" · hero "Lock In Your Survey Date" + "Call Graeme: 0404 870 868" · stakes section "Book a Pre-Pour Check" · service cards "Quote My Construction Job" / "Price My Civil Works" / "Set Out My New Home" · differentiator "Check My Build Against the Plans" · process "Send Us Your Drawings" · FAQ "Ask Graeme a Question" · form "Get My Survey Quote".

Patterns that work:
- **First person** ("Quote My Job", "Get My Survey Quote"): the visitor is telling themselves what they'll get.
- **The specific job or outcome** ("Book a Pre-Pour Check", "Set Out My New Home", "Check My Build Against the Plans").
- **A human** ("Call Graeme", "Ask Graeme a Question") for owner-operated businesses.
- **The real next step** ("Send Us Your Drawings", "Lock In Your Date", "Book Your First Session").
- **Different verbs:** book, lock in, quote, price, check, send, ask, start, see, plan, claim.

Rules:

- Start with a verb. Say what they get: "Get a Free Quote", "Book Your Initial Consultation", "Request a Site Survey".
- Add value or remove risk where it's **true for them**: "Free", "No obligation", "15-minute", "Same-week" (only if stated on their site).
- Micro-CTAs on service cards match the service: "Get a Civil Quote", "Book an Assessment".
- Never: "Submit", "Click here", "Read more" (as a primary), "Contact us" (as the main CTA), "Learn more" repeated on every card.
- Show the phone number as a button or bold link in the header for any business where people phone.

## 3. Default section flows by industry

**Local trades / construction services**
Header (logo, nav, phone, Get a Quote) → Hero (outcome headline, service area, a creative primary CTA such as "Lock In Your Survey Date" + Call [name]) → Trust bar (licences, insurance, years, who they work for) → Cost of getting it wrong / value → Services with micro-CTAs → Proof (Google reviews) → Process (3 steps: enquire → on site → result) → About (local owner, experience) → Service areas → FAQ (pricing, turnaround, coverage) → Final CTA with short quote form + phone → Footer with hours and contact.

**Health / allied health**
Header (Book, phone) → Hero (who it helps, reassurance, Book) → Trust bar (registrations, years, locations, wait time only if stated) → Who we help / conditions → Services → Practitioners → Reviews → What to expect at your first visit → Fees / rebates / referrals FAQ → Book CTA + phone → Footer with locations and hours.

**Professional services / coaching**
Header (Book a Call) → Hero (the outcome, who it's for, Book) → Credibility bar (media, qualifications, client logos) → Problem / pain → Services as outcomes → How it works → About the person → Testimonials / results (real) → FAQ → Final CTA → Footer.

**B2B / SaaS / platforms**
Header (Book a Demo) → Hero (outcome for the team, Book a Demo + Explore) → Logo/partner bar → Problem → Solution / products with screenshots → How it works → Results / case studies (real) → Security / support / local presence → FAQ → Demo CTA → Footer.

**Food & drink producers (wholesale + stockists)**
Header (Enquire About Wholesale) → Hero (what they make + where it comes from, wholesale CTA + Find a Stockist) → Proof bar (named farms/suppliers, press) → Two paths: chefs/trade vs shoppers → Range with real product names and a micro-CTA per category → Provenance (the farms they buy from) → The maker → Press (+ reviews) → Stockists list grouped by area → FAQ (ordering, visiting, ingredients) → Wholesale enquiry form → Footer.

**Retail / e-commerce**
Header (search, cart, Shop) → Hero (hero product/category, Shop) → Trust strip (delivery, returns, reviews, payment options — only as offered) → Categories → Best sellers → Reviews → Brand story → Newsletter / offer → Footer.

## 4. Trust signals buyers look for

| Industry | Signals to look for on their site / Google |
|---|---|
| Trades & construction | Licences, insurance, years, service area, Google reviews, named projects, who they work for |
| Health | AHPRA / association membership, practitioner qualifications, locations, wait times, rebates, reviews |
| Professional services | Qualifications, media, client logos, case results, testimonials |
| B2B / SaaS | Client and partner logos, case studies, integrations, security, local support |
| Retail | Review count, delivery and returns terms, secure payment, brand story |
| Food & drink producers | Named farms and suppliers, press, the stockist list, the maker's background, awards |

Place the strongest signal **directly under the hero**, real reviews in their own section before the final CTA, and a micro proof line next to the main CTA.

## 5. Psychology principles to apply and name in the handover

| Principle | How it shows in the design |
|---|---|
| **Clarity (5-second test)** | Hero says what / who / why / next in one glance |
| **Social proof** | Real star rating near CTAs, review section before the final CTA |
| **Authority** | Licences, years, credentials, partner logos high on the page |
| **Choice reduction (Hick's law)** | One primary action; secondary is visibly quieter |
| **Friction reduction** | Short forms (3–6 fields), click-to-call, clear next step |
| **Uncertainty removal** | "How it works" steps and FAQ answer the "what happens next?" |
| **Specificity** | Concrete services and outcomes instead of generic claims |
| **Loss aversion / cost of inaction** | Show what getting it wrong costs (true to the industry) |
| **Visual hierarchy (Fitts' law)** | Large, high-contrast primary buttons where the eye lands |
| **Consistency** | Same CTA wording throughout builds a single clear path |

## 6. CRO self-check (must pass before delivery)

- [ ] Hero passes the 5-second test (what / who / why / next)
- [ ] Clear primary goal, with a steady header button, and **creative, varied, section-specific wording** on every other button (no two in-page buttons say the same thing)
- [ ] Every CTA starts with a verb and states the benefit; no "Submit / Click here / Read more"
- [ ] Phone is a visible header CTA where calls matter
- [ ] Trust bar directly below the hero
- [ ] Real reviews shown with stars, count and verbatim quotes, or a clearly labelled review placeholder listed in ITEMS TO VERIFY
- [ ] At least one CTA visible every second section
- [ ] Process / "what happens next" section present
- [ ] Objections answered (FAQ or equivalent) before the final CTA
- [ ] Final CTA section with short form + phone
- [ ] Business-critical details kept (hours, service area, prices, offers, licences)
- [ ] Exact brand colours used (logo-measured primary), real logo and photos, no repeated photo
- [ ] Nothing invented; every fact traceable to their site or a source you opened
- [ ] Before & After clearly shows the stronger CTAs and flow
